AV1 output through SVT-AV1

2026-11-15 · task #40 · measured on an Apple M1 Pro (16 GB), deps 1.16.0 bundle (FFmpeg 9.0.2, SVT-AV1 v4.2.0); the macOS x64 FFmpeg (evermeet 9.0.1) run under Rosetta for F1 only

Outcome: built. A new output codec, VideoCodec::Av1 (libsvtav1), in MP4 and MKV, at 8- or 10-bit 4:2:0. Anything else is converted in the script, which is field-aware and dithered. Left to FFmpeg, the two fields' chroma of an interlaced picture were blended (F4). HDR10 static metadata travels in -svtav1-params (F5). The defaults are preset 6 (about x264 medium's speed at SD, faster at 1080p, F6) and CRF 24 (about x264 CRF 18 by VMAF, at 21–40% less bitrate, F7). The macOS x64 FFmpeg has no SVT-AV1 (F1). There the app shows AV1 as unavailable and the worker refuses it with a clear message, until a deps release ships an x64 FFmpeg that has it.

Correction (2026-11-15, task #44): deps 1.17.0 keeps evermeet's FFmpeg, so the exception now covers macOS x64 at every deps version; the test still fails if that bundle gains libsvtav1.

libsvtav1 declares two pixel formats, yuv420p and yuv420p10le. That is SVT-AV1's main profile: no 4:2:2, no 4:4:4, no 12-bit. VideoToolbox has no AV1 encoder (Apple decodes AV1 but cannot encode it).

Why SVT-AV1 and not libaom or rav1e: it is fast enough to sit behind QTGMC (F6), and it is the encoder HandBrake, FastFlix and StaxRip lead with. Offering libaom on Intel Macs instead would add a second option vocabulary at a fraction of the speed.

F3 · What FFmpeg does with a format SVT-AV1 can't take

InputStored, left to FFmpeg
yuv422p, yuv444pyuv420p
yuv422p10le, yuv420p12leyuv420p10le

It works silently, through swscale: a progressive conversion with no dither of the script's choosing. That is the trap ProRes and v210 fell into before (ProRes script chroma, v210). So VideoJob::output_chroma_subsampling resolves AV1's limit in the script.

  • A selection, or under Match source a pipe format, that is already 4:2:0 at ≤ 10 bits is kept.
  • Anything else becomes 4:2:0 at 8 bits for 8-bit content, or at 10 bits for anything deeper.

The -pix_fmt stays as a guard that asks for exactly what the script delivers.

AV1 itself has no interlaced coding, so a picture left interlaced goes out flagged progressive (field_order_signal = None, and the job log says so), as with x265.

F5 · HDR10 static metadata

-svtav1-params "mastering-display=G(0.265,0.69)B(0.15,0.06)R(0.68,0.32)WP(0.3127,0.329)L(1000,0.0001):content-light=1000,400" reads back from the stream as the same values. SVT stores chromaticities in 1/65536 and luminance in 1/256 and 1/16384. For example, red_x = 44564/65536 = 0.68000, max luminance = 1000, min = 2/16384 ≈ 0.00012, MaxCLL 1000 / MaxFALL 400. HdrMetadata::svtav1_params converts from the x265 units the worker stores (0.00002 chromaticity, 0.0001 cd/m²) to SVT's decimals. integration_hdr_test.dart runs the real job on the HDR10 fixture.

Default: preset 6. It is about x264 medium's speed at SD and faster at 1080p, and it is far above QTGMC's own rate (~15 fps at 1080i Very Slow on this machine), so it rarely limits a job. Preset 4 saves another ~13% of size for ~35–40% more time. Presets 1–10 are offered. 0 and below take hours a minute, and SVT-AV1 itself warns that 11 and up are "meant for automation tooling… visual artifacts may occur".

Limits: Apple Silicon with NEON. SVT-AV1 on an AVX2 x86 is relatively faster against x264, but this was not measured. One clip per resolution. VMAF is saturated on this soft VHS picture, so read the bitrate column.

Default: CRF 24.

  • On VHS it is slightly above x264 CRF 18's VMAF at 40% less bitrate.
  • On the small camera clip it is 0.6 VMAF below at 21% less. Matching VMAF there takes CRF ~20 at the same bitrate as x264.

The plan's provisional 28 was too low a quality to stand in for VapourBox's x264 default. Switching to AV1 from another codec family sets the quality to 24 (EncodingSettings.withCodec), because CRF numbers mean different things across encoders.

Limits: VMAF (v0.6.1) models one viewing condition and is weak on grain and on soft sources (VHS saturates near 100). Two short clips are not a population, and the camera clip is 320×240 and already lossy. The AV1 gain grows with resolution and clean detail, so these numbers are conservative for HD restorations.

Correction (2026-11-15, during the work). The first run paired the 29.97 fps camera clip's frames by timestamp. It scored every encode around 70, x264 at CRF 18 included, with a minimum PSNR of 14 dB: the pairs were misaligned. VMAF now pairs by frame index (setpts=N), and the numbers above come from that run.

Decisions

  • SVT-AV1 only, software only. Hardware AV1 (av1_nvenc RTX 40+, av1_qsv, av1_amf RX 7000+) has its own quality scales (AMF's AV1 QP runs 0–255) and can't be verified on any machine or runner here, so it is left for a follow-up.
  • Its own encoder family (EncoderFamily::SvtAv1). Its numeric presets share no vocabulary with x264's, and the settings dialog resets the preset only on a family change.
  • Quality clamped to 0–63 in the worker, because saved presets and imported jobs can carry anything.
  • SVT_LOG=2 on the encoder process. SVT-AV1 prints a ~20-line Svt[info] banner to stderr on every encode, which would crowd the stderr tail that a failure is reported from.
  • A missing libsvtav1 is refused before the pipeline starts (one ffmpeg -encoders call), and the app shows the codec as unavailable through the same detector as the hardware encoders.
  • No built-in preset switches to AV1. H.264 stays for compatibility and FFV1 for archiving.

Not tested

  • Windows and Linux AV1 encodes on real machines. The CI push gate checks that the encoder is present, and nightly runs the heavy encodes.
  • Playback of the files on TVs and phones. AV1 decoding needs recent hardware: Apple M3/A17, Intel Arc/11th gen, RTX 30, RX 6000 and newer, or a software decoder.
  • Sharp HD content for F7; film-grain synthesis (film-grain=N); SVT's tune options.
