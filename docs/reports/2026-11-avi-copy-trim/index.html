A Passthrough trim into AVI runs its sound late

2026-11-15 · macOS arm64, deps 1.16.0 (FFmpeg 9.0.2) · task #24, follow-up to the AVI audio seek (task #22)

Outcome: fixed. With Passthrough audio and a trim, the encoder seeks the source's sound with an input -ss and copies it. A stream copy keeps the packets from where the demuxer's seek lands, the keyframe before the in-point. Matroska, MP4 and MOV store that pre-roll as a later start of the video and stay in sync. AVI cannot store where a stream starts, so the pre-roll played under the first picture and the whole track ran late: 76–375 ms on these fixtures (a keyframe every 0.4 s), 667 ms on avi_h264_bframes.avi at frame 20 (one keyframe), and a whole GOP at worst.

For a seeked Passthrough copy into AVI (audio_restoration::needs_sound_cut):

  • Linear PCM (pcm_u8, s16le, s24le, s32le, f32le, f64le) is re-written in its own codec. Seeked and decoded, ffmpeg trims it to the sample: the output is the source sample for sample (max difference 0), in sync to the sample.
  • Compressed sound (MP3, AC-3, AAC, …) is still copied, its bits untouched, from the packet whose start is nearest the in-point: a second input of the same file with -copypriorss:a 0, seeked half a packet early only when the stream's time base is finer than a packet (table 1). Within half a packet (≤ 16 ms for AC-3), and from an AVI source exactly where MKV puts it.
  • MKV, MP4, MOV, Convert, None and untrimmed jobs keep every argument.

Keeping the codec over decoding compressed sound to PCM was the user's choice: the copy loses nothing and lands within half a packet; decoding would be exact but changes the codec.

Method

Fixtures (built by the scripts, nothing committed): the first 12 s of small_clip.mp4's picture (H.264, a keyframe every 0.4 s from 0.3 s), stream-copied, with a 12 s chirp (150 → 3030 Hz, never repeating) as PCM, MP3 and AC-3 in AVI, AAC in MP4 and AC-3 in MKV; and a two-stream AVI (PCM + AC-3). Trims from frames 20, 100 and 251 (0.67, 3.34, 8.37 s). Task 22's chirp copy of avi_h264_bframes.avi (a decoder delay, one keyframe) from frames 1–3 and 20.

Sound vs picture is where the source's sound at the in-point plays against the output's first picture (+ = late): half a second of chirp, cross-correlated at 8 kHz against a decode of the source. It reads within ±0.4 ms of an exact match (seen on bit-identical outputs, where the in-point falls between 8 kHz samples), so read anything inside that as in sync. Max diff compares the output from its first picture with the source from the in-point, a second of each, decoded at the source's own rate: 0 is bit-identical.

Script: scripts/ffmpeg_candidates.py.

  • Today's copy is late by the pre-roll in AVI only. MP4 and MKV store it (≤ 1 ms).
  • Decoding PCM is exact and bit-identical; re-writing it in its own codec is lossless.
  • -copypriorss 0 drops the copy's packets before the seek, but how depends on the stream's time base: ffmpeg drops a packet whose timestamp, in that time base, is below the seek rounded to it. An AVI's compressed audio counts in packets (MP3 3/125, AC-3 4/125), so the rounding alone keeps the packet nearest the in-point, exactly where MKV and a full decode put it; seeking half a packet early there kept the one before (+19 to +27 ms). A fine time base (MP4 1/48000, MKV 1/1000) keeps the first packet at or after the seek (up to a packet early, −21 ms), and the half-packet-early seek is what finds the nearest. So the worker seeks early only for a stream whose time base is under a quarter of its packet.
  • From an AVI source, MP3 and AC-3 are limited by the demuxer's own timestamps (−9 to +9 ms here, in MKV, MP4 and a full decode alike; task 22 saw the same on MP3). Not this fault, and not changed.

Considered and not taken

  • Decode every copied stream to PCM. Exact for all codecs, but the source's MP3/AC-3/AAC would no longer be passed through. Kept as the answer for PCM only, where it costs nothing.
  • Warn only. A GOP of desync is not something to leave in a file.
  • Delay the picture instead. AVI pads a video gap with null frames only after the first packet (avienc), so the first picture would be held from 0 over the pre-roll's sound. The same muxer behaviour affects an untrimmed late-video source to AVI: task #30.
  • Pre-trimming the NUT copy at the in-point (task 22) rebased its first packet to 0 and landed 29 ms late in MKV.

Limits

  • macOS arm64, FFmpeg 9.0.2 only. The rounding in -copypriorss is ffmpeg's muxer code, the same in the 9.0 series every platform pins; nightly runs the heavy test on the others.
  • One picture source (a 0.4 s GOP) and one delayed AVI. A longer GOP only makes today's lag bigger; the cut does not depend on it.
  • Copying compressed sound from mid-stream starts its decoder cold: MP3's bit reservoir or AAC's priming may colour the first frame. The cross-correlation, half a second in, does not see it.
  • A source mixing a packet-counted and a fine-time-base compressed stream would get one early seek for both; no single container produces that.
  • Big-endian, DVD and Blu-ray PCM cannot go into AVI at all (ffmpeg refuses the header), and A-law/µ-law are copied by packet; neither changed.
  • The probe reads the first 256 audio packets for durations; a stream whose packets change length later is cut by its early packets' length.
