Issue #148 — Frame Rate combined with Noise Reduction and Sharpen

2026-11-10 · issue #148 · measured on an Apple M4 Pro (macOS arm64), deps 1.14.0 bundle, against the templates of app 1.6.0

Outcome: not reproduced; no pipeline change made. The reporter saw "glitches" and "black shadow-like patches" once Noise Reduction and Sharpen were added to a frame rate conversion that was clean on its own. On macOS arm64, across 5 denoisers × 4 sharpeners × FlowFPS/RIFE × 8/10-bit, plus a fast pan and hard cuts, adding Noise Reduction and Sharpen never measurably increased dark patches in the synthesized frames. Every cell sits at the same ~0.001–0.005% noise floor; a single black 32×32 block would be 1.3%. The three code-level suspects were each tested and none holds up here (below). The likeliest remaining explanations are platform-specific (Windows MVTools v24, or RIFE on a particular GPU/driver) or content-specific, and both need the reporter's clip, method and GPU. A nightly regression test now pins the measured behaviour (app/test/integration_frame_rate_combined_test.dart).

1. What runs, and in what order

In the generated script: Noise Reduction → … → Resize → Sharpen (after resize, issue #109) → Film Grain → Frame Rate (last video pass). Denoise, then sharpen, then interpolate is the conventional order, so a pass-order bug was not the lead. What adding the two passes changes is the input the interpolator sees.

The probes do not copy the frame-rate code. They extract the {{#FRAME_RATE}} block from worker/templates/pipeline_template.vpy, fill its placeholders as the worker does (block 16, overlap 8, 29.97 → 59.94), and exec it after haf.SMDegrain / haf.LSFmod etc. at the app's defaults. RIFE runs through MoltenVK on the M4 Pro GPU.

2. The metric

For each synthesized frame (every odd frame at 2×), the share of luma pixels darker than both real frames either side of it by more than 24/255. An interpolator has nothing to build such a pixel from, so "darker than both" is the signature of the reported black patches. A brighter-than-both share is recorded alongside it as a control.

What it can't tell you: motion alone produces such pixels at moving edges (see the pan, §4), so it compares configurations; it is not an absolute defect count. It sees luma only. One 40-frame film clip is not a population, and Apple Silicon running MVTools master is not Windows running v24.

3. Hypotheses tested

H1 — RIFE fed out-of-range RGB by the sharpener

The RIFE block converts YUV to RGBS and passes it on unclamped; a sharpener's overshoot could push samples below 0, outside what the network was trained on. Measured (scripts/range.py): the share of RGB samples below −0.05 in the worst frame.

SMDegrain + LSFmod−0.1030.9041.436%

Weak. The unfiltered source already sends 1.2% of samples below −0.05 into RIFE; sharpening adds about a sixth to that, and the matrix (§4) shows RIFE's output unaffected either way. Clamping before RIFE was the planned fix for this hypothesis and was not made: with no measurable effect it would only change synthesized frames for every user on speculation.

H2 — FlowFPS's motion search misled by denoised/sharpened detail

Both mv.Analyse calls search the same unfiltered super clip. Flattened texture or sharpening halos could mislead block matching, and FlowFPS warps along whatever vectors it gets. Not supported: FlowFPS's dark share with any denoiser or sharpener is within the noise of FlowFPS alone (§4), and the frames look the same (§5). A prefiltered search clip was the planned fix; it was not made, because it would change every FlowFPS job's output with no measured defect to justify it.

H3 — FlowFPS wrong above 8 bits

A >8-bit source (or 4:2:0 at >8-bit, converted to YUV422P16 by the block) runs FlowFPS at high depth. Measured (scripts/depth.py): FlowFPS on the same clip at 10 and 16 bits differs from 8-bit by a mean 0.13/255 (worst frame 0.29) and 0.14/255 (worst 0.32). Ruled out on arm64 (MVTools master). Windows and macOS x64 ship the v24 release, which was not tested.

6. Corrections made during the work

  • The plan assumed the 10-bit fixture pal-sd-25.mov would test real motion at depth. It is static colour bars, so every metric on it was exactly 0, which proves nothing. The 10-bit column above uses the film clip converted to 10-bit instead.
  • The plan named H1 (RIFE input range) the most likely cause. The range measurement made it the least supported: the source alone is already out of range by nearly as much.

7. What wasn't tested

  • Windows x64 and macOS x64, which ship the MVTools v24 release rather than master. FlowFPS's frame count already differs between the two (issue #129); its pixels may too. This is the likeliest place for the bug to be.
  • RIFE on NVIDIA/AMD/Intel Vulkan drivers. RIFE ran on Apple GPU via MoltenVK only.
  • KNLMeansCL — the machine measured had no usable OpenCL device in this session. BM3D, mClean, TemporalDegrain2 and the rest of the denoisers weren't measured.
  • Combinations with QTGMC (double-rate deinterlace before the retime), resize, or other passes; the reporter listed only "FPS", "Denoise" and "Sharpness".
  • The preview path, and the encoder: everything here is the script's output, upstream of ffmpeg. The regression test encodes losslessly (FFV1) for the same reason.
  • The reporter's own footage. None was supplied.
  • The full app pipeline, end to end. No Rust toolchain was available to build a 1.6.0 worker on the machine measured, so the probes exec the template block directly. The new regression test drives the real worker and will run in nightly.yml; it was type-checked (flutter analyze) but not executed before commit.

8. Decisions

  • No change to the Frame Rate, Noise Reduction or Sharpen passes. Both candidate fixes (clamp before RIFE; prefiltered FlowFPS search) would alter output for every frame-rate job, and neither addresses anything measured.
  • A nightly regression test, app/test/integration_frame_rate_combined_test.dart: for FlowFPS and RIFE (RIFE where the GPU probe says it runs), the dark share with default Noise Reduction + Sharpen must stay within 0.05 percentage points of frame rate alone. That tolerance is ~10× the noise floor and ~25× below a single black block, so it fails on the reported defect without flaking on sharpening contrast. It runs on all four nightly platforms, which includes MVTools v24 on Windows and macOS x64 and RIFE on the Windows/Linux runners: the coverage this report lacks.
  • Ask the reporter for a short clip, the Frame Rate method and target, the Noise Reduction and Sharpen methods, the OS and the GPU, linking this report.

run.sh decodes 40 frames of Tests/TestResources/small_clip.mp4 (from 0 s and from 30 s) into the scratch directory and runs range.py, depth.py, matrix.py, stress.py and images.py with the bundle's Python. harness.py holds the loader, the template extraction and the metric. The RIFE paths it fills in are the macOS bundle layout.
