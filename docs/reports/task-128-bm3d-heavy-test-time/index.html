The BM3D smoke encode that outgrew the 300 s job limit

Task #128, 2026-11-28. The failure: integration_new_passes_test.dart › noise reduction: BM3D runs end-to-end, chroma included, TimeoutException: nr_bm3d timed out after 300s on the macos-arm64 heavy leg of nightly 46018310484 (job 140571075844, a deps_run_id dispatch of task #114's deps build). The worker log there shows the encode finished, 79/79 frames at 0.27 fps (ffmpeg elapsed=0:04:49), just as the job limit expired. The evidence is the logs of the last 40 nightly.yml runs (scripts/bm3d_times.py, data/bm3d_times.txt).

Outcome

  • The test sat at the limit on every Mac, not on one draw. The test allows 6 minutes, but it called WorkerHarness.runJob without a timeout:, so the harness's default job limit, 5 minutes, applied. The encode was the whole fixture, interlaced_test.avi (720x576, 79 frames), through V-BM3D at radius 1 with chroma: on macos-arm64 it took 222–300+ s, and the job limit killed it on 5 of 21 legs; on the x64 Macs 4 of 47.
  • Not the deps. On an M1, task #114's bundle and rc4's run the template's BM3D block at the same speed (32.2 s vs 33.3 s for 24 frames, measured in task #114), and the arm64 heavy step's total time was unchanged.
  • Fix (the test): 24 frames, an explicit job limit, a frame count. The job is trimmed to frames 0–23 (startFrame: 0, so no seek is added). V-BM3D at radius 1 still has a neighbour each side, and both steps, every plane and the 16-bit round trip still run. runJob gets timeout: 5 min, below the test's 6, so a real hang is still named and sampled by the harness (task #99). The test asserts the output has 24 frames, so a trim that stopped applying could not bring the long run back unnoticed. No retry.
  • Expected time: ~3.6 s a frame on the slowest Mac legs (79 frames in 280–300 s), so ~85–90 s for 24, over 3x inside the job limit. Not yet measured on the runners (see "Not tested").

Measured: the test's time on every heavy leg

Tests in one file run one after another, so the BM3D test's time is its result line's timestamp minus the previous test's (TTempSmooth). Limits of the metric: it includes a few seconds of ffprobe and test overhead, and a timeout row is a floor (~300 s, or up to 320 s with the hang sampling), not the encode's real time. Two Linux rows where the whole file failed in setUpAll (6 s and 13 s) are left out; they are not BM3D.

Local run after the change

Apple M1, deps 1.18.0-rc5, debug worker: the test passed, 24 frames out, in 95 s. That time is not representative: the machine's load average was 135 on 8 cores during the run (another job's VapourSynth). It shows the trim, the count and the job limit work, not the speed.

Decisions

  • Both a trim and an explicit limit, not one. A larger limit alone (~12 min) would have kept ~4.5 minutes of arm64 nightly time on one smoke test, which checks only that BM3D runs. A trim alone would have left the test on the default, against CLAUDE.md's rule that a slow heavy test states its timeout.
  • 24 frames: the M1 measurement in the task used 24, and it leaves BM3D's temporal step real neighbours on both sides with room to spare.
  • The harness's default stays at 5 minutes: raising it would hide hangs in every other test.
  • Never a retry.

Not tested

  • The new time on the CI runners: the first nightly after the change gives it. Re-run scripts/bm3d_times.py then; with the trim, the delta it prints is the 24-frame test's time.
  • Other timeouts in the same logs (many 30-second test timeouts in other files, a macOS x64 plugin-format probe child at 3600 s) were not examined; they are not this test.

Reproduce

python3 scripts/bm3d_times.py --runs 40 > data/bm3d_times.txt with an authenticated gh; job logs are cached in $BM3D_LOG_DIR (a temporary directory otherwise). Newer runs push older ones out of the 40, so the rows will differ.
