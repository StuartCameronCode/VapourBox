Unblend (SRestore): recovering frames from blended frame-rate conversions

2026-11-14 · requested on the videohelp forum (thread 419911, post #152) · measured on an Apple Silicon Mac (macOS arm64) against the deps 1.16.0 bundle (VapourSynth R78, havsfunc r31), on a machine shared with other work (load average up to 42), so times are relative, never absolute

Outcome. havsfunc's srestore, which ships in the bundle, does nothing useful on VapourSynth R78: its detector keeps its history in variables that only advance when it is called for consecutive frames, it relied on std.Cache(make_linear=True) to make that happen, and R78 ignores make_linear. Its output is identical to plain decimation. A VapourBox port (worker/templates/srestore.py) runs upstream's per-frame arithmetic unchanged (300/300 decisions identical to havsfunc driven frame by frame), but makes every decision a pure function of the 48 frames before it, so the preview, the encode and any thread count agree. On fixtures with a known answer it recovers 143/143 film frames from PAL blending (plain decimation: 87) and 101/125 PAL frames from NTSC blending (decimation: 73), at the deinterlacer's cost plus a few percent. Built, as its own pass, Unblend (SRestore), right after Deinterlace.

1. What was asked, and the plan

SRestore deblends footage that was frame-rate converted by blending: 23.976 fps film on PAL through a standards converter, a PAL programme converted to NTSC. Every movement shows a double image that no deinterlacer removes. The usual chain is double-rate QTGMC, then srestore(frate=…).

The plan was to probe havsfunc's srestore before building on it: its ~30 nonlocal variables make the output depend on the order frames are requested, which threatens preview = encode; its output length comes from its own numr/denm arithmetic; and it measures an 8-bit copy of the picture.

2. havsfunc's srestore on R78 is plain decimation

srestore ends with ChangeFPS(core.std.Cache(last, make_linear=True), …). R78 prints:

Critical: Explicitly instantiated a Cache with make_linear set. This is no longer possible and the
original clip has been passed through instead which may cause severe issues.

Its detector's history (seven frames of blend, clear, difference and motion measurements, plus a phase tracker) only advances when jmp = lfr + 1 == n, i.e. when it is called for the frame after the last one it saw; otherwise every register is reset to the current frame's value. ChangeFPS only requests the frames it keeps, so the detector never sees two in a row (scripts/requests.py, on the deinterlaced film fixture through vspipe):

Concurrent requestsDetector callsjmp trueFrames evaluated
114300, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, …
814300, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, …

So its output is exactly what decimation gives (Section 5, both fixtures: the same picks, frame for frame). srestore is the only function in havsfunc that uses make_linear or keeps state across FrameEval calls, so nothing else in the templates is affected.

3. The port: upstream's arithmetic, a fixed window

worker/templates/srestore.py splits upstream into three parts:

  • Measurements (stats_clip): upstream's own, on an 8-bit copy of the luma (downscaled, frame differences, the blend expression, three PlaneStats), gathered as four frame properties on a 1x1 clip.
  • The detector (_Detector.step): upstream's srestore_inside for an integer omode (the default 6), line for line, minus the clip output. The "pp0".."pp3" post-processors synthesize pixels and are not ported.
  • The output: the decision for frame n replays the detector with fresh state over frames max(origin, n − 48) … n; the frame it names is taken from one of a few pre-built shifted copies of the clip (no node per frame), and SelectEvery with exact integer offsets decimates to the target.

Faithfulness (scripts/parity.py): driven linearly (every frame, in order, one thread), the port and an instrumented havsfunc make 300/300 identical decisions on both deinterlaced fixtures. havsfunc there runs its expression through akarin (VapourBox's ARM patch), so this also covers the Expr implementation.

Why a window, not pre-roll or forced linear access

The detector's phase tracker (offs, lpos) does not forget where it started. In the plan stage, on a frame-level synthetic fixture, starting fresh K frames back still gave a different decision from the sequential run at 36% of frames with K = 160 (95% with K = 0). So neither "pre-roll the preview until the state converges" nor "make the encode linear" can make a preview equal the encode. Pictures converge much faster than the state does (Section 5). Making the decision a pure function of a window, used by both the encode and the preview, gives equality by construction.

Those plan-stage numbers came from a cruder fixture (no interlacing, no QTGMC) and are superseded by Section 5 for recovery; they are kept here because they are what ruled pre-roll out.

4. Fixtures with a known answer

Tests/TestResources/generate_blended_test.sh (FFmpeg only) builds two:

  • blended_film_50i.mkv: small_clip frames 1350–1493 taken as 23.976p film, repeated 2:3 to 59.94 (as telecine does), blended to 50p by linear interpolation between the two nearest frames (framerate), interlaced to 25i TFF. Unblended to 23.976 it should give back the 144 frames.
  • blended_pal_5994i.mkv: frames 1350–1474 as 25p PAL, blended to 59.94p and interlaced to 29.97i, at 10-bit 4:2:2. Unblended to 25 it should give back the 125 frames.

Both are x264 at CRF 10, lossy on purpose. "Clean" means within 1.77 (film) / 1.72 (PAL) luma levels of an original frame (±4 positions searched). That limit is the 99th percentile + 0.25 of the same chain with frames repeated instead of blended (an unblended twin, built by score.py), whose frames sit 1.04–1.07 levels from their originals at the median. A 50/50 blend of neighbours sits 3.5–3.6 levels from either original at the median, but only 0.28 at the 10th percentile: where nothing moves, a blend is clean, and the metric counts it so.

What the metric can't tell you: the fixtures are synthetic linear blends of one clip, the easy case. A real standards converter blends fields with its own filters and noise. No real blended capture was available. Recovery on real tapes will be lower, and how much lower is not known.

6. Order, threads and the preview

scripts/order.py:

  • The encode through vspipe at 1 concurrent request and at the default: byte-identical Y4M, both fixtures.
  • Temporal reach of one output frame: 46 input frames back (the replay starts 48 back, but its oldest measurement reads frames from two later) and 4 ahead.
  • Preview emulation (decode a window around the target, pad it with copies of its first frame so its index modulo the ratio's period is the encode's, tell the detector where real frames start): identical to the encode at all 42 targets.
Correction. The first run used a window radius of 46 (the measured reach) and mismatched 6 of 22 film targets. The replay starts at n − 48, and that index has to be in the window even though its pixels are not read, so the radius must be at least the window. The worker's FrameMap radius is the window plus 8.

In the app, ProcessingPipeline::preview_output computes the padding and the detector origin for Unblend as it does for the Frame Rate pass (both, when both are on). The heavy test (integration_unblend_test.dart) previews five source frames of the film fixture through the real worker and finds each identical to its encode frame (difference 0.00).

7. Length, rates and refusals

srestore computes its own ratio (numr/denm) in floating point from the input and target rates. scripts/lengths.py sweeps 10 source rates × 15 targets (named and custom): 89 pairs where srestore decimates and its ratio is the exact target ratio. In every one, the port's output is exactly floor(n × num / den) frames at exactly the target rate, at every length tried from 1 to 2401 frames. A clip so short it would give no frame at all is refused with an error. The other 61 are refused by the worker (and raise in the script):

  • 48 where srestore would not decimate: the target is not below the input rate, or below a fifth of it;
  • 13 where srestore's ratio only approximates the target, all NTSC-to-integer ones, e.g. 59.94 → 50 (835/1001 instead of 1001/1200) or 29.97 → 25.

Every conversion the pass is for is accepted: 50 → 23.976 / 24 / 25 / 29.97 and 59.94 → 23.976 / 24 / 25 / 29.97. The table is data/ratio_table.json, and the Rust port of the ratio arithmetic (unblend_parameters.rs) is tested against every row of it.

8. Depth, Expr implementation, cost

scripts/depth_speed.py and scripts/cache.py:

  • 8 vs 10-bit: the film fixture after QTGMC, shifted exactly to 10-bit: identical measurements, identical decisions, outputs identical after converting back. Through the worker, a 10-bit copy of the source gives the 8-bit encode's frames exactly. The PAL fixture is 10-bit 4:2:2 throughout.
  • Expr: the one expression goes through the usual _expr() helper (akarin on ARM). akarin and VapourSynth's own std.Expr give bit-identical measurements on both fixtures; this was checked because the detector thresholds them.
  • Cost: the first version tripled the time of an SD encode. Each decision reads its window through 49 shifted copies of the measurement clip, VapourSynth's automatic cache sizing evicted those frames, and with them the QTGMC frames behind them. 300 QTGMC frames were computed 799–894 times (up to 14 times each). Pinning the measurement clip's cache (std.SetVideoCache, 1x1 frames) fixes it:

QTGMC + Unblend (143 frames out)2.41–2.46 s299

One preview frame at SD took 0.43–1.32 s across runs on the loaded machine, against 0.04–0.07 s for QTGMC alone with its own small window. The window has to be decoded and deinterlaced, about 28 source frames either side.

9. Decisions

  • Not havsfunc's srestore. It decimates and nothing more on R78 (Section 2).
  • A window of 48, the same in encode and preview. Pure by construction; recovery on both fixtures at least the linear run's.
  • Its own pass right after Deinterlace, not a Frame Rate method. The Frame Rate pass runs last, after denoising (which averages blends into clean frames), sharpening and grain (which add frame-to-frame differences the detector reads), and resizing. The detector has to see the blends as the deinterlacer leaves them. As a separate pass it also combines with a later Frame Rate conversion. Decided with the user.
  • Only the target is exposed. thresh, mode, omode and speed stay at upstream's defaults: nothing here showed a fixture another value fixes.
  • Unreachable targets are refused with one wording, shown by the panel too; combinations that work but waste it (Deinterlace off, single rate, IVTC) get advice, never a block.
  • No built-in preset turns it on. Blending is a property of one particular conversion, and on footage that was never blended the pass only decimates (judder).

10. Not tested

  • A real blended capture (none was available; the fixtures are synthetic linear blends).
  • x86, Windows and Linux: the port is pure Python and core filters, and the decisions do not depend on the Expr implementation, but every number here is from macOS arm64. The nightly heavy run covers the other platforms.
  • Upstream's other modes (omode 1–5 and the pp post-processors, mode 3, speed).
  • Blends that are not between neighbouring frames, and sources whose cadence changes mid-clip (edits after the conversion).
