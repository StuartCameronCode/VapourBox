Passthrough into AVI: a primed sound put a null frame at the head

2026-11-15 · macOS arm64, deps 1.16.0 (FFmpeg 9.0.2) · task #43, found by task #41; the Passthrough half of task #38, fixed with the cut of task #24

Outcome: fixed. A sound that ffmpeg encoded into MKV or MP4 (AAC, MP3, AC-3, MP2) starts with the encoder's start-up, its priming, at a negative timestamp (AAC −21.3 ms, MP3 −23.0 ms, AC-3 −5 ms, MP2 −10 ms). MKV and MP4 mark it to be skipped; AVI cannot store a negative timestamp. An untrimmed Passthrough into AVI copied the sound with no cut, because task #24's cut only applied when the encoder seeks. ffmpeg's default -avoid_negative_ts then shifted every stream by the priming. For AAC and MP3 that wrote a null frame at the head (101 stored frames for 100), and the player played the priming as silence. So the sound was AAC −18.6 ms, MP3 −16.9 ms against the picture. AC-3 and MP2 kept their frames but ran +5.4 / +10.1 ms.

The copy is now cut at the head the same way a trim is cut: from the packet nearest the first picture, through -copypriorss:a 0 with its threshold moved half a packet early by -itsoffset on the packet input. The results:

  • AAC and MP3 store 100 frames and sit at +0.08 ms and −0.9 ms. MP3's next packet starts 47 samples after 0.
  • AC-3 and MP2 keep the packet they start with, which is the nearest one, and so keep their timing.
  • No output option is added, and every other job keeps its arguments byte for byte.

The cost is the head of the sound. The decoder now starts without the dropped packet: AAC fades in over 21 ms, and MP3 is silent for one packet and at half level for the next. Before, the same span played 21–23 ms of silence.

Fixtures and method

scripts/fixtures.sh makes sources with no gap between sound and picture: 25 fps, 100 pictures, white frames at 25 and 75, and a 1 kHz beep at 1 s and 3 s over a 300 Hz bed, mono. The sound is encoded by ffmpeg at 128 kbps into MKV (millisecond timestamps) or MP4 (an edit list), at 48 or 44.1 kHz. A FLAC master is the unprimed control. small_clip.mp4 (29.97 fps, stereo AAC) is a real source whose AAC starts 5.1 ms before 0, a partial priming. The heavy test uses two committed fixtures from Tests/TestResources/generate_primed_sound_test.sh: primed_aac.mkv (96 kbps, below task #41's block-size overshoot) and primed_mp3.mp4.

The measurements:

  • Stored frames is the AVI header's avih.dwTotalFrames (scripts/riff.py), null frames included.
  • Lag is the beep's onset, counted in decoded samples, minus the white frame's presentation time (scripts/measure.py, both from task #38's report).
  • Offset is the output's decoded sound against the source's own decode, by cross-correlation over 0.5–1.5 s (scripts/xcorr.py). The head RMS is the level of the first windows of the output's decoded sound.

Limits: every number is from macOS arm64 with FFmpeg 9.0.2, and the players measured are ffmpeg's decode only. The cut is at a packet, so the sync is good to half a packet at best: 10.7 ms for AAC, 12 ms for MP3, 16 ms for AC-3.

What each candidate does:

  • B, the first idea in the task, stops the shift but leaves the sound late by the whole priming, which is a bigger error than today's.
  • C drops every packet before 0. That is right for AAC and MP3, whose next packet starts at or just after 0. It is wrong for AC-3 and MP2, whose first packet is mostly real sound: they lose 27 and 14 ms.
  • G drops only packets more than half a packet before 0. That keeps the packet nearest 0, the same rule as a trim's cut. Every packet G keeps is at or after 0 once offset, so the muxer never shifts anything. Adding disabled (E) or an IVTC job's options (H, I) changes nothing.
  • avienc ignores where an audio stream starts: the first audio pts of every output above is 0.000.

Two more controls. small_clip.mp4 stores 3151 frames under A, B, C and G alike. 60 fps MP2 and AC-3 sources store 240 of 240 frames under A and G: a 5–10 ms priming against a 16.7 ms frame.

The offset is the output's sound against the source's own decode, so it leaves out the null frame. With A, the AAC picture is also 40 ms late, which gives the −18.6 ms lag above.

Copied into AVI, the priming plays as silence: 21 ms for AAC and 23 ms for MP3. That is the encoder's start-up, which MKV and MP4 skip. Cutting at the nearest packet removes that silence, but the decoder then starts without the dropped packet's overlap (and, for MP3, without its bit reservoir). So AAC fades in over its first packet, and MP3 is silent for one packet and at about half level for the next. The span is the same order as before, and the sound is now in sync.

Decisions

  • The cut, not the output option. -avoid_negative_ts disabled alone trades the null frame for sound late by the whole priming. With the cut, no packet reaches the muxer with a negative timestamp, so no output option is needed, and an IVTC job's make_zero is untouched.
  • The nearest packet, not 0. The cut uses the same rule as a trim's, so AC-3 and MP2 keep the packet they start with.
  • One threshold rule. sound_cut_input_args moves -copypriorss's threshold half a packet before the sound's start. It uses -ss when that point is inside the file, and otherwise -itsoffset by the remainder; it never uses both, since task #38 found -itsoffset inert beside -ss. This also changes a trim whose sound starts within half a packet of 0: it used to cut at 0, and now cuts at the nearest packet.
  • When the cut applies. It applies when a copied, compressed stream's first packet is before the format's start, by more than 1 µs (SoundCut::starts_early). It applies even when nothing would be dropped, as with small_clip.mp4, so that no AVI job hands avienc a negative audio timestamp at any frame rate. PCM never starts early, and a stream replaced by the restored track is not copied.
  • One decision. sound_cut_applies is needs_sound_cut (a trim, unchanged) or may_cut_sound_head plus the probe. A failed probe is a warning, never a refusal.
  • Not covered here: TS sources already carry the priming in their video start offset, which is the trimmed cut. AVI sources store no negative timestamps.
