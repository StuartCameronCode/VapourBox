Field order for the other codecs: FFV1, HuffYUV, FFVHuff, x264, x265

2026-11-14 · follow-up to v210 output and interlaced field-order flagging · measured on Apple Silicon (macOS arm64), deps 1.16.0 bundle (FFmpeg 9.0), VLC 3.0.23

Outcome: built for five codecs, deliberately not for the rest. vspipe's Y4M header always says Ip, so before this change every codec but ProRes and v210 wrote a picture left interlaced as progressive.
  • FFV1, HuffYUV, FFVHuff now get -top 1|0 on the pipe input, as v210 does: MKV FieldOrder tb/bt, MOV fiel 0x0209/0x020e, pixels bit-identical. FFV1 also writes it in its own bitstream, so it survives even in AVI.
  • x264 now codes the picture interlaced (MBAFF: -flags +ildct -x264-params tff=1|bff=1). At equal bitrate it beat coding the woven frame as progressive on all three fixtures: +0.33 to +1.00 dB field PSNR on VHS, +0.07 to +0.18 dB on broadcast; BD-rate −16.5 % / −2.2 %. It costs ~20 % encode time, and Apple's hardware decoder refuses MBAFF.
  • x265 and every hardware encoder are left unflagged. x265 has no interlaced coding, and a container-only flag on a progressive bitstream is read by some players and ignored by others. The job log now says so whenever a picture left interlaced goes out unflagged.

Method

The source is Tests/TestResources/interlaced_test.avi (720x576 25i VHS capture, TFF by idet), decoded to a Y4M as vspipe pipes it (header Ip, 4:2:0). Each codec is encoded with and without -top, in each container, and read back four ways: ffprobe's stream field_order, the MOV fiel atom's bytes (by walking the box tree, scripts/fiel.py), the decoded frame flags, and the bitstream headers (FFmpeg's trace_headers). Player behaviour: AVFoundation's format description and a full AVAssetReader decode (scripts/fields.swift, decode.swift), and VLC's automatic deinterlace. Quality: scripts/quality.sh, below.

Without -top every row reads progressive (MOV 0100). FFV1 carries the field order in its own frame header, which is why its frame flags follow -top in every container; HuffYUV and FFVHuff have no such field, so in AVI nothing records it, and the job log says so.

-top alone changes nothing in an x264 stream (in MKV the container says tb over a progressive bitstream). +ildct makes it MBAFF, but libx264 starts from its default top-field-first and only follows the frames' flags later: a BFF picture's SEI said top-first on 46 of 79 frames. Naming the order in -x264-params makes every frame agree, at identical size and pixels (the BFF encodes with and without bff=1 decode to the same MD5). MBAFF works at every format the app can deliver: High 10, High 4:2:2 and High 4:4:4 Predictive were each checked.

P3. x265

All three decode to the same pixels. x265 has no interlaced coding: its interlace mode declares that each picture it is given is one field, so a woven frame becomes a "field" of twice the height, and a decoder that honours it shows the wrong thing. -top alone flags the container over a progressive bitstream; the next two sections show players disagree about such a file.

P4. Hardware encoders (VideoToolbox, the ones available here)

h264_videotoolbox ignores +ildct (bitstream progressive, frame_mbs_only_flag=1); hevc_videotoolbox, like x265, only gains a container flag. NVENC, QSV and AMF could not be run: left unflagged rather than flagged on a guess. VideoToolbox's decoder refuses x264's MBAFF stream ("hwaccel initialisation returned error"); FFmpeg falls back to software and the result is identical.

The split that decided x265: with a container-only flag, AVFoundation (QuickTime, Final Cut) treats the file as interlaced and VLC (FFmpeg's decoders) as progressive. One file, two answers, and neither is the bitstream's. For HuffYUV/FFVHuff the same split is acceptable: they are editing intermediates, the flag costs nothing, and the editors they are made for read the container. VLC follows the bitstream (MBAFF, FFV1) and ignores the container.

Q1–Q3. Does interlaced coding pay for itself in x264?

Progressive coding of the woven frame (what every H.264 encode of a picture left interlaced was before) against MBAFF, preset medium, three fixtures: vhs (above, 79 frames), dvbt (pal-dvbt-fieldcoded-25i.ts, 720x576 25i broadcast, 125 frames) and bff (vhs shifted up one line, which swaps the field order: idet BFF 75/75). Quality is luma PSNR per field: encode and source are both split with separatefields, so both are judged on the pictures a deinterlacer sees.

SSIM moves the same way or not at all (e.g. vhs 2000 kbit/s 0.9665 → 0.9684; dvbt ≤ 0.0005). Limits: PSNR rewards softness and three clips are not a population; all three are SD PAL, no 1080i was measured. The bff fixture is the vhs picture shifted, so it confirms the BFF path, not a second source.

Q3, time: one thread, CRF 18, median of three: 2.67 s progressive, 3.21 s MBAFF (+20 %). At CRF the MBAFF file is also slightly smaller (vhs CRF 18: 1.83 vs 1.86 MB) and better (41.27 vs 40.83 dB).

Decisions

  • FFV1, HuffYUV, FFVHuff: flag (-top). Pure metadata, lossless, read by editors. In AVI only FFV1 keeps it; the job log says so for the other two.
  • x264: code interlaced. Smaller or equal for the same quality on every fixture, and the bitstream, container and frame flags all agree, which a flag alone could not give. The cost is ~20 % encode time and software decoding on Apple hardware; a picture left interlaced is an explicit choice (deinterlacing is on by default), and one that will be played on a TV or deinterlaced later.
  • -x264-params tff=1|bff=1, not +ildct alone, for the BFF SEI trap above. A Custom FFmpeg Argument -x264-params comes later and replaces it: the stream stays MBAFF; only a BFF picture's SEI goes back to disagreeing.
  • x265: nothing. Its interlace mode misdescribes the picture, and a container-only flag gives players different answers about one file. Deinterlace, or choose H.264.
  • Hardware encoders: nothing. VideoToolbox can't code fields; NVENC, QSV and AMF couldn't be checked. Revisit with hardware.
  • Say it, don't refuse it. field_order_note writes a job-log line whenever a picture left interlaced goes out unflagged. The user may want exactly that file.
  • Exhaustive per codec. VideoCodec::field_order_signal has no catch-all arm, so a new codec cannot be added without this decision.

Verification

  • Rust: test_field_order_args_matrix sweeps every codec × MP4/MOV/MKV/AVI × six job shapes (deinterlaced, TFF/BFF, progressive, Stabilize pitch), against a per-codec expectation written out separately from field_order_signal; test_field_order_note the log line; field_order_signal_per_codec the families.
  • Heavy: app/test/integration_field_order_test.dart encodes through the worker and checks MKV/MOV/AVI for FFV1, HuffYUV, FFVHuff and v210 (field_order, fiel bytes, FFV1's frame flags, the AVI log line, lossless frames bit-exact); ProRes's container and frame header; x264's frame_mbs_only_flag, MBAFF and pic_struct on all ten frames at TFF and BFF; x265 unflagged with the log line; deinterlaced jobs progressive.
Correction during review. The heavy test's lossless check first failed on every FFV1/HuffYUV/FFVHuff case: the source AVI has null frames (stored frames 0, 5, 6, …), which the reference decode skipped while the worker repeats into the gaps. The flag was never the cause; the reference now decodes with -fps_mode cfr, as the worker does.

Not tested

  • NVENC, QSV and AMF (no hardware here), and Windows/Linux FFmpeg builds (the nightly heavy run covers the flags there).
  • Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or a TV opening the files. The player evidence is AVFoundation and VLC.
  • 1080i sources, and x264 presets other than medium.
