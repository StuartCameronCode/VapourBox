Task #55: the timestamp-reset scan no longer blocks analysis

2026-11-17 · follows task #51's report (2026-11-packet-index-seek, §3 and §9) and task #21's (2026-11-timestamp-resets) · measured on an Apple M-series Mac (macOS arm64), deps 1.16.0 (FFmpeg 9.0.2), debug workers built from dc9e36c (before) and this branch (after)

Outcome: built. Analysis of an MPEG-PS/TS no longer waits for its whole-file timestamp-reset scan. The worker's model now comes back without the scan (--probe-source --defer-scan). The scan runs afterwards in the background (--complete-source), one source at a time, with progress. On a 1 GB program stream on the internal SSD, Video Info, frame 0 and the processed preview of frame 0 now wait for 0.35 s instead of 0.98 s. On a network share the old wait was 17.3 s/GB (77 s for #51's 2 h DVD title). The new wait reads only the head and tail of the file, but was not measured on the share (§4).

Anything that seeks waits for the scan's answer: thumbnails, the scrubber, in/out points, zoom, the detectors and jobs. A seek is not safe anywhere in an unscanned file, not even before a reset (§2), so the task's "block only seeks past a reset" could not be built as stated. When a reset is found, the join now uses the model's scan instead of reading the source a second time: 2.7 s instead of 4.2 s for 2 GB on the SSD.

1. What analysis waited for

Since task #50, --probe-source runs four probes in parallel threads. One of them, the reset scan (ffprobe -show_entries packet=stream_index,dts_time), reads every byte of a TS/PS. Analysis waited for the slowest probe, and on a TS/PS that is the scan. Task #51 measured it on a 4.48 GB PAL DVD title on an SMB share at about 60 MB/s: 17.3 s/GB cold, the speed of cat (16.5 s/GB). The link is the limit, not ffprobe. So nothing about an item appeared for about 77 s: not Video Info, not frame 0, not the deinterlace auto-switch.

A source that does reset was read three times in full:

  1. by the probe's scan;
  2. by --make-continuous, which scanned again because it was given nothing;
  3. by the copy itself.

2. Why seeks wait for the whole scan

The task suggested blocking only jobs and seeks past a reset, perhaps with a frontier that grows as the scan reads. Task #21's own measurements rule that out. On seek_reset_h264.ts (the reset is at picture 150), a seek to frame 140 delivered pictures from the second segment, from 290 on (2026-11-timestamp-resets, §2). The cause is how libavformat finds a time in a TS/PS: it bisects the file by bytes, and every timestamp occurs once per segment. So the search can settle in any segment, whichever side of the reset the target is on. Until the whole file has been scanned, no seek is known to be safe.

What is safe is a decode from the start without -copyts. ffmpeg's discontinuity correction joins the segments there, which is exactly what the joined copy relies on. So while the scan runs:

Runs at onceWaits for the scan's answer
  • Video Info (from the model);
  • the soft-telecine flags (the first 30 frames);
  • idet, which on a TS/PS now decodes from the start and drops its first 2 s by frame count (trim=start_frame=50 at 25 fps) instead of -ss 2;
  • the frame-0 Original (frame 0 never seeks);
  • the processed preview of frame 0;
  • the pass list;
  • the deinterlace auto-switch.
  • Thumbnails;
  • the scrubber, in/out points and zoom (the controls do nothing, and the timeline says why, with the scan's progress);
  • Crop Detect and Descale Detect (they wait, then run on whatever the item is decoded from);
  • noise / halo;
  • jobs. The queue waits with "Waiting for the timestamp check of …", and Cancel stops the wait.

When the answer is "no reset" (the common case: the one real DVD title measured so far had none), the model is completed and the timeline opens. When the answer is "reset", the join runs as before, the copy is probed, Video Info is read again from it (the frame count changes), and then the timeline opens on the copy. The original is never seeked.

3. Measurements on the internal SSD

There was no long DVD or broadcast file on the internal disk, so these use a synthetic one, built by scripts/run.sh: 16 minutes of PAL MPEG-2 at 8 Mbit/s, GOP 12, with AC-3 sound, 0.995 GB. The reset file is the same stream twice over (1.99 GB), so its clock goes back at the join. The file was in the page cache (it had just been written), so these are CPU-bound times and the share's are not. Each figure is the median of 3 runs.

The synthetic stream has fewer packets per GB than the real title (#51 measured 1.58 CPU s/GB for the real title's scan, 0.9 here), so the real title's absolute times will be higher.

4. On the network share: not measured

The share measurement did not run. The SMB share holding #51's title was mounted when this work started. It was gone (unmounted) when the measurement began, and it can't be remounted without the owner's credentials. scripts/analysis_probe.py share is written and ready. It rotates the title's four 1 GB VOB parts across the measures, as #51 did, and flags any read faster than 5 s/GB as cached. Rerun run.sh with T55_VOBS set to fill this section.

What can be said without it:

  • Before: 17.3 s/GB cold for the scan (#51's measurement of the same code path), so 77 s for the 2 h title before anything showed.
  • After, unmeasured: before anything shows, the share serves only the probes that read the head and tail of the file:
    • the seek plan's 20 s of packets;
    • the video start's first 64 packets;
    • the last 3 s for the untimed last picture;
    • idet's first ~10 s;
    • frame 0.
    #51 measured the first 1 000 packets of the title at 0.49 s on the share. These reads are a few tens of MB, so a few seconds at 60 MB/s is the expectation, not a measurement.
  • The scan itself still takes the whole read (about 17 s/GB), now in the background with progress, and jobs still wait for it. Deferring moves the wait off analysis; it doesn't remove it.
  • Not measured: whether the background scan slows the next video's analysis or the user's frame-0 previews over the same link (the script's contention step).

5. Decisions

DecisionReason
Strict gate: nothing seeks until the scan answers (the plan's option A; the user did not choose before Implement, so the recommendation was taken)The constraint "decodePath correct before any decode that could cross a reset", given §2. The alternative was provisional thumbnails, redone (and trims cleared) when a reset turns up. It shows the timeline sooner, but it shows the wrong frames on a file that resets.
The scan starts after the item's analysis, not during itThe analysis's own reads aren't fighting the scan for the link; and a join can't swap decodePath under an analysis that is still reading the original.
One scan at a time across the queueOn a share, two scans would only share the link.
The worker completes the model (--complete-source); the app never edits the model's JSON"A new source fact goes in the model, computed in the worker" (task #50). A deferred model completed this way is byte-identical to one probed in one go: checked on four fixtures, two of them with a reset, by the Rust and heavy Dart tests.

6. Checked

  • Rust (source_continuity unit tests, continuity_integration_test.rs):
    • the line-by-line scanner equals the old whole-list rule;
    • positions are read and never go back;
    • a deferred model completes, with rising progress ending at 1.0, to exactly the model --probe-source gives;
    • a non-TS/PS model is identical with or without --defer-scan;
    • --make-continuous --source uses the model's scan only when the model describes the file, and makes the same copy.
  • Dart, push gate (timestamp_continuity_deferred_test.dart). The worker, preview generator and field-order detector are faked and record every decode. It checks that:
    • while the scan is pending, Video Info, frame 0 and the frame-0 preview arrive and nothing seeks: scrubber, in/out points, zoom, Crop Detect and the job are all held;
    • after "no reset" the timeline opens and the held detector and job run;
    • after "reset" the join gets the model, Video Info is read from the copy, and the only seeking decode is of the copy;
    • a failed scan warns;
    • removing the video stops its scan;
    • Cancel during the wait returns to idle;
    • scans run one at a time;
    • idet's arguments are as described in §2.
  • Heavy (integration_deferred_reset_scan_test.dart): the same chain through the real worker on both reset fixtures. A cancelled scan returns nothing, and an MP4 has nothing to defer.
  • Killing the worker mid-scan leaves no ffprobe running. ffprobe dies on its next write to the closed pipe (checked on a 4 GB file).

7. Limits and what wasn't tested

  • The network share, cold reads, and contention (§4). This is the case the task was about, and its "after" is an expectation, not a measurement.
  • Windows and Linux: not tried.
    • Whether ffprobe exits when the worker is killed: checked on macOS only.
    • The app's Process.kill of the worker.
  • Synthetic sources: one synthetic stream (testsrc2 with noise), not a real DVD or a broadcast capture; the reset is a byte-for-byte concatenation.
  • Not looked at in the running app: no screenshot of the waiting state. The waiting state's text was checked through the view model only.
  • The DVD import's output is still scanned twice (by the import, then by analysis). It is local temp, and now off the critical path.
