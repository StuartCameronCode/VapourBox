Audio restoration: where the sound moves, and what each step does

2026-11-08 · ffmpeg 9.0.2 (deps bundle, macOS arm64) · deep-filter 0.5.6 · Apple Silicon · signals generated with ffmpeg's lavfi sources · scripts in scripts/ (run.sh reproduces every number)

Question. VapourBox copied or re-encoded audio and never processed it. The Audio Restoration pass adds channel fixes, a sync offset, drift correction, declip/declick, rumble and hum removal, broadband denoise (afftdn), DeepFilterNet speech enhancement, loudness normalisation and a limiter. Before building it on ffmpeg's filters: which of them move the sound in time, which defaults are wrong for this use, and does each actually do its job on a known signal?

Outcome: built. Four filters move or mis-handle the audio with their defaults; each is handled in the one place every filter is chosen (AudioPlan::new, worker/src/audio_restoration.rs):

  • afftdn outputs its input 25 ms late at every rate and strength → trimmed with atrim=start_sample; its default noise floor (nf=-50) barely touched real hiss → the source's measured floor is passed; its noise tracking (tn=1) undid that → off.
  • atempo moved every click ~20 ms early on top of the stretch → drift is corrected by an exact resample instead.
  • alimiter delays by its attack and normalises back up to 0 dBFS by default → latency=1:level=0.
  • Rendering to a WAV loses a late-starting audio stream's gap → aresample=first_pts=0 first.

DeepFilterNet (an add-on) delays by 30 ms; its own -D compensates. The architecture decision — render the soundtrack once, before the video encode, rather than filtering in the encode — is what DeepFilterNet, two-pass loudness, negative offsets and subtitle alignment all need.

afftdn's delay is exactly 25 ms (rate / 40 samples) whatever its strength — Video-Enhancer's 1200 samples at 48 kHz is the same number. The plan trims round(rate × 0.025) samples at the source's own rate (1103 at 44.1 kHz; the measured 1102 is the correlation peak's integer position, within a sample).

alimiter's default also normalises: a click track peaking at −1.34 dBFS, limited to −6 dB, came out peaking at 0.00 dBFS with defaults and −6.02 dBFS with level=0:latency=1.

The brief said atempo; the measurement says no. Drift between a capture's audio and its picture is a sample-clock error, so resampling is the exact inverse — it restores the original pitch along with the length (at most 0.5% by default, about 9 cents). asetrate only takes an integer rate, which straight from 48 kHz would be off by up to 1e-5 (37 ms over an hour), so the plan upsamples to the rate between 4× and 8× where round(r1 × factor) / r1 is closest to the factor — within ~1e-11 (resample_ratio, unit-tested to under 0.4 ms an hour). The heavy test measures the encoded result: a 20 s picture with 20.08 s of audio puts every click within 2 ms of t / 1.004.

2. afftdn's noise floor

White noise at −38.8 dBFS RMS with clicks; the noise level measured in a click-free stretch after afftdn has adapted (tempo_denoise_probe.py).

afftdn=nr=20:nf=-39 (measured floor)−52.4 dB13.6 dB
afftdn=nr=20:nf=-39:tn=1−39.6 dB0.7 dB

Correction, kept visible: the first build passed nf=-50:tn=1, reasoning that noise tracking would find the floor itself. The first heavy-test run showed 0.7 dB of reduction. Tracking does not rescue a wrong floor here — it defeats a right one.

The floor afftdn is given is the source's: the RMS of the quietest tenth of 50 ms windows in a minute from the middle of the window, decoded at full bandwidth (white hiss is 8 dB quieter below 4 kHz than across 24, so an 8 kHz analysis would underestimate it), clamped to −80…−30 dB. The upper clamp guards against content with no pauses (continuous music), whose "floor" is the content itself.

Automatic hum detection reads the same minute as the noise floor: Goertzel power at 50/100/150 and 60/120/180 Hz, each in 2 s blocks (0.5 Hz resolution, so mains wandering by a tenth of a hertz stays in the bin), against six neighbours 4–7 Hz away. A line 12 dB above its neighbours counts; whichever mains frequency stands out more wins; nothing standing out means nothing is notched (and the job log says so). Unit tests cover 50.1 Hz hum with harmonics under noise and a tone, 60 Hz hum whose third harmonic dominates, and clean audio.

What this metric can't tell you. The signal is a test tone with a speech-like envelope, not speech. 5. Decisions

  • Render the soundtrack before the encode (one WAV, 32-bit float so adeclip's rebuilt peaks and later gain don't clip), and map it into the encode — never an -af on the encode. DeepFilterNet is a separate program; two-pass loudness must measure the whole track first; a negative offset needs audio from before the trim point; and the subtitles transcribe this file, which is the output's audio sample for sample, so their cues follow offset, drift and trim by construction. (Heavy test: a 2 s offset moved Whisper's cues by a median of 2.3 s — its timestamps are coarse.)
  • One decision site, fixed order, one -af per ffmpeg run — the encoder's -vf rule, for the same reason. Order: start padding → channels → declip → declick → rumble → hum → afftdn (+ its trim) → drift resample → lead silence → [DeepFilterNet] → loudness → limiter → length. Asserted for all 512 combinations of the toggles, with no, positive and negative offsets.
  • Drift factor = whole source audio length / whole encoded picture length, the picture's from output frames over output rate (FrameMap), applied only above 0.05 s and up to a cap (0.5% default) — a bigger mismatch is a missing stretch or the wrong track, not drift.
  • Offset and drift compose on the source timeline: source time = (in-point + t − offset) × factor. A positive offset from the start becomes silence; a negative one reads later — never adelay alone.
  • The track is cut or padded to the picture's exact length.
  • One stream restored, first in the output; the others copied untouched with their own tags (naming one stream's -map_metadata stopped ffmpeg copying the rest's — found by the heavy test). With audio set to copy, the restored stream is encoded (FLAC/MKV, AAC/MP4·MOV, PCM/AVI). Since changed: beside an editing intermediate it is PCM in every container, v210 from 2026-11-10 and ProRes from 2026-11-14 (report).
  • DeepFilterNet as a manifest add-on, upstream's own v0.5.6 binaries pinned by hash (MIT OR Apache-2.0). macOS builds need macOS 13 (their minos); the arm64 build is linker-signed, the x64 build unsigned — the installer signs a program that fails codesign --verify.
  • No preview: the preview is picture only, and the panel says so.

6. Not tested

  • Windows and Linux (the filters are ffmpeg's, the same 9.0 series everywhere; DeepFilterNet's Windows, Linux musl and Linux aarch64 binaries never ran here), and the macOS x64 DeepFilterNet build (needs macOS 13 on Intel).
  • Real recordings: every number here is from generated signals. Hum detection on real ground-loop hum with a strong bass line, the noise-floor estimate on content without pauses, and DeepFilterNet on real dialogue all deserve a listen.
  • Very long sources: drift accuracy was measured over 20 s; the resample ratio's accuracy is computed (≤0.4 ms an hour), not measured over hours.
  • Sources with more than two channels: the channel fix applies to stereo only and says so; the other steps run on every channel.
