A late-starting picture in AVI became a 600 fps file

2026-11-15 · macOS arm64, deps 1.16.0 (FFmpeg 9.0.2) · task #30, found during the AVI copy trim (task #24); corrects the video start offset report

Outcome: fixed. When a source's sound starts before its picture (broadcast captures: 1.0–1.3 s), an untrimmed encode keeps that layout: the encoder delays the picture by the gap (VideoJob::output_video_delay) and copies the sound from the start. MKV, MP4 and MOV store the delay as the video stream's start. AVI has no per-stream start: avienc gave the video a 1/600 time base and filled the gaps between pictures with null frames, and since it pads only after the first packet, the first picture was written at 0 and held until the second (1.0–1.24 s). Sync after the first picture was right; the file was not: an editor read 600 fps and 30× the real frame count.

AVI output now starts sound and picture together at the first picture, as a trim does: output_video_delay is 0 for a container that cannot store where a stream starts (ContainerFormat::stores_stream_start), the sound is read from the first picture, and a copied sound is cut by task #24's machinery. The 1.0–1.2 s of sound before the first picture is left out of an AVI (the job log and the Audio Restoration panel say so); MKV, MP4 and MOV are unchanged and keep it.

Fixtures and method

The late-video fixtures of Tests/TestResources/generate_video_start_offset.sh: 25 fps, 100 pictures, the video starting 1 s after the sound, white frames at pictures 25 and 75 with a beep at each (late_video_intra.ts and late_video_gop.ts with MP2, late_video.mp4 with AAC, late_video.mkv with FLAC), and the real DVB-T capture pal-dvbt-fieldcoded-25i.ts (1.202 s: an open GOP's B pictures before the 2.722 s keyframe; no beeps). Every job runs through the worker, untrimmed, Passthrough sound. Sync is measured against the file's own content, not its start_time tags: lag = the beep's onset minus the white frame's presentation time, both decoded from the output (scripts/measure.py). Frame counts are the AVI header's stored count (null frames included) and the frames a decoder returns.

The MP4 source's AVI timeline was 21.3 ms later throughout before the fix (AVI drops the AAC priming edit); picture and sound moved together, so sync was unaffected. A copied compressed sound is cut at the packet nearest the first picture, so its lag is bounded by half a packet (MP2 24 ms, AAC 21.3 ms, FLAC ~21 ms): +2.1 and −2.6 ms here. PCM and the restored track are cut to the sample.

A is a one-condition change to the one decision. Every consumer already reads output_video_delay and handles a sound start above 0: the sound's input seek, task #24's sound cut (which already applied to "Start the sound with the picture" on a late video), Audio Restoration's OutputWindow, transcription and rule 9's sound copy.

What changed

  • VideoJob::output_video_delay returns 0 when !container.stores_stream_start(); lead_sound_left_out / lead_sound_note say how much sound an AVI leaves out, logged once per job.
  • Task #24's log wording no longer assumes a trim ("for the AVI output", "nearest the in-point or first picture").
  • App: ContainerFormat.storesStreamStart (twin); the Audio Restoration notice says an AVI leaves the gap out; the pass list no longer suggests Audio Restoration for the gap when the output is AVI.
  • Tests: Rust (every container × Passthrough/Convert; MKV/MP4/MOV keep the delay; AVI without a gap unchanged) and the heavy integration_video_start_offset_test.dart: every late fixture to AVI with FFV1 and HuffYUV (25/1, 100 stored frames, frame 0 at 0, frames 0/1/99 = source frames), sync copied (half a packet + 1 ms) and restored (1 ms), and the DVB-T capture (126 stored frames).

Limits

  • macOS arm64 only; avienc is the same FFmpeg 9.0 series on every platform, and the nightly runs cover them.
  • A copied compressed sound is bounded by half a packet (task #24), not exact.
  • The head sound is left out of AVI by design; option B is the way to keep it, at the costs above.
  • Found on the way and not fixed here: Convert into AVI writes AAC, and the AVI then holds a null frame at the head and its sound ~18.6 ms early, on every source, gap or not (the same AAC-priming effect as option B's MP4 row). Filed as task #38.
