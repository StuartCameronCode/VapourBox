DVD import: the title, not the title set

Task #27 · 2026-11-15 · worker dvd_reader.rs

Summary

extract_title took the title it was given only to find its title set (VTS), then copied every sector of that VTS's title VOBs. A disc that keeps several titles in one VTS (a TV series' episodes, a film and its extras) gave every title on every import; a multi-angle title came out with all its angles interleaved; --chapters was parsed and ignored. Task #21's join made such a file decode end to end, but it was still the wrong content.

Fixed: the import now copies exactly the cells the title plays: chapter by chapter from the VTS's PTT table, each chapter's program cells from its PGC, one cell of each angle block (angle 1 by default, --angle N), the chapter range if one is asked for. Cells in an interleaved block (seamless angles, seamless branching), whose sector ranges span the other angles' units, are copied VOBU by VOBU by following each NAV pack's next_vobu, as libdvdread's own play_title.c does. The picker's chapter and title durations now come from the same cell list (they counted every angle's cell, and only the first chapter's PGC).

Title 1: red, 50 frames. Title 2: three chapters, grey 60 x25, grey 120 x50, grey 180 x25. Title 3: blue, 75 frames. 320 KB, committed.

Before the fix every case is the whole title set, 225 frames, in disc order. After it each output is exactly its title's or range's pictures, in order. Title 2 crosses two VOB IDs, and title 2 chapters 2-3 one, so their clocks reset and #21's join ran: the decoded timestamps count straight through (dvd_extract_integration_test asserts it). Without libdvdread, the unit test fixture_titles_are_copied_apart reads the same VOBs directly and checks that the three titles' sectors add up to the title set exactly (nothing lost, nothing twice) and that each output's NAV packs name only its own VOB IDs.

Limit of the metric: solid-colour chapters show which content came out and in what order; they can't show a corrupted picture inside a chapter. Frame counts and the per-frame colour runs together pin the boundaries to the frame.

What was not tested

  • Angles and interleaved cells on real media. dvdauthor cannot author a multi-angle or seamless-branching title (its XML has no angle element; man dvdauthor lists "angle" only as a menu entry and a register), and no such disc was at hand. The cell selection and the NAV walk are covered by synthetic unit tests: an angle block of three (angle 1 and 3 picked, angle 4 refused), interleaved cells flagged and planned as a walk, and an in-memory disc of two angles interleaved in units of two VOBUs whose walk returns exactly one angle's sectors. The DSI offsets were checked against libdvdread 6.1.3's nav_types.h; the tests are built on the same constants, so a wrong constant would pass them. Extracting both angles of a real multi-angle DVD and comparing them would close this.
  • Encrypted discs. Nothing in the read path changed but the offsets: reads still go through libdvdread's DVDReadBlocks on the title VOBs, which decrypts with libdvdcss when present.
  • Windows and Linux: the integration test runs there in the push gate (every deps bundle carries libdvdread); it was run here on macOS arm64 only.

Decisions

  • No fallback to the whole title set. A title whose IFO can't be mapped to cells now fails with the reason. Copying the VTS was the bug; doing it quietly on odd discs would keep it.
  • Cells chapter by chapter through the PTT table, not "the first PGC's cells": that also covers titles of one PGC per chapter and chapters listed out of program order (both unit-tested).
  • Range copy for ordinary cells, NAV walk only for interleaved ones. An ordinary cell's sector range is exactly its content; walking it would add a dependence on every NAV pack being intact for no gain.
  • Touching cells merge into one read; a cell played twice is copied twice, as a player shows it twice.
  • Bad-sector handling is unchanged: a read that fails after data was written stops the copy with a warning, as before.
  • IFOs read from the filesystem, as enumeration already did (they are never encrypted), so a bad chapter range or angle is refused before libdvdread is loaded.
  • The app's picker still offers no chapter or angle choice; the worker and DvdService carry both.
