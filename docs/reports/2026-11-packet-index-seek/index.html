Task #51: position decodes by a packet index instead of time seeks?

2026-11-16 · probe R3c of task #42's plan · measured on an Apple M-series Mac (macOS arm64, 16 GB), deps 1.16.0 (FFmpeg 9.0.2), this branch's debug worker, a 2 h 16 min PAL DVD on an SMB share (≈ 60 MB/s) · the Mac was shared with another task's test run throughout (load average 30–126), so wall times on the internal disk are noisy and CPU times are given beside them

Outcome: against, as a replacement for the per-container corrections. An index of every video packet (PTS, key flag, byte position) is cheap to add where a whole-file pass already happens: folded into today's timestamp-reset scan of a TS/PS it costs the same on a network share (17.5 vs 17.3 s/GB, both bound by the link) and 1.10× the scan's packet pass in CPU with integer timestamps. Starting the decoder at key packet K's byte position is exact on every start frame of every TS/PS fixture (and at 10/50/95 % of the 2 h title), so it can replace the SeekPlan GOP preroll (C1). But that is the only correction it retires. The AVI decoder delay (C2) and the count near the start (C4) survive: an MPEG-2 AVI stores its I and P packets with no PTS and drops an open GOP's leading B pictures after a seek, so counting from K was right on 13 of 90 starts. The microsecond round-up (C3), the frame-0 exception (C6) and the joined copy (C7) survive too. The grid lookback (C5) would go only by indexing MP4/MOV/MKV/AVI, a whole-file read those formats do not pay today (45 s for a 2.6 GB MKV on the share). Net: about 103 lines of Rust and 38 of tests retired for about 105 new, against the criterion of C1 and C3/C4 retired with code removed.

Separately: today's reset scan of the 2 h title on the share reads the whole 4.48 GB before analysis shows anything: 77 s, at the link's speed. That is a live cost of the current code, not of the index; filed as its own task (see §9).

1. The question

Today a decoder reaches frame N with a time seek and then corrects for the container. Each correction was its own task. The idea (task #42's plan, R3c): at analysis, ffprobe -show_packets lists every video packet's PTS, DTS, key flag and byte position without decoding; frame N becomes "start decoding at key packet K, keep the picture whose PTS is pN". The decision criteria were fixed in the plan before measuring (§7).

The first cold pass (one run per probe, before the rotation) measured the merged index at 39.5 s/GB and the video-only index at 37.0, against 20.4 for the scan: it looked as if the index doubled the cost on a share. It did not reproduce in 12 rotated runs (all 16.3–17.5 s/GB) nor on two other files (17.4, 16.0); the first pass coincided with the other task's load starting. Those four rows stay in data/cost.json marked "round": "first".

The index replaces the scan's pass rather than adding one. For MKV/MP4/MOV/AVI it is all new cost: they have no scan today, and the whole file is read.

4. Correctness (B)

Every start frame of every fixture of integration_seek_alignment_test.dart and integration_source_grid_test.dart, 3 pictures each, named by the MD5 of their luma against frame N as the app numbers it (the untrimmed decode, today's frame-0 arguments; on every grid source it equals issue #174's independent placement by timestamp, checked). The control is today's decode_start, rebuilt in Python (today_args.py) and checked against the 35 cases of seek_arg_cases.json and source_grid_vectors.json that the Rust and Dart unit tests pin, its constants and probe arguments against source_decode.rs itself.

  • M1: -copyts -ss K's PTS (floored to the µs), the grid filters to slot N. K = the last key packet whose own fps-filter slot is ≤ N, so the picture slot N shows has a PTS ≥ K's: it replaces the 8-slot lookback. No seek (M0) when K is the first key.
  • M2 (TS/PS): the same with -skip_initial_bytes pos(K) instead of -ss. On a PS with no grid (packets without PTS, so no grid today: both DVD fixtures and the real title), K is the last key at or before frame N's time and today's absolute trim selects (M1-trim / M2-trim).
  • count (AVI that stores decode order): K by DTS, then drop N − k pictures. Seeks 3/23 s past K's DTS without accurate seek, because ffmpeg starts every seek of a file with a delayed stream 3/23 s early (ffmpeg_demux.c's dts heuristic); count-raw omits that.

What the failures are

  • M1 on the DVB-T TS (88 of 128 wrong): targets 31–55 all came out as 56, 57, 58, targets 59–73 as 84 … — the time search lands mid-GOP and decoding starts at the next keyframe, exactly #169's fault. On TS/PS only the byte start is safe. (M1-trim on the two small PS fixtures was exact; not relied on.)
  • count on the MPEG-2 AVI: always 2 late from the second GOP on (target 14 → 16). The packets show why: N/A,13,330540,K — K is decode picture 13, displayed as 16; the leading B pictures 14 and 15 of its open GOP need the previous GOP's P picture, and of them only one comes out. A packet list cannot say which picture a decode from K shows first.
  • count-raw: without the 3/23 s compensation, every seek lands a key early on any delayed source (H.264 AVI 30/90).

The 2 h title and the frame count

At frames 20 441, 102 209 and 194 197 (10/50/95 %), today's arguments and M2 both gave the 5 pictures a separate decode from three keys earlier names by PTS (-f framemd5, first picture + N frames): exact, both. The index's frame count (last picture's slot + 1 on a grid, packets otherwise) agreed with the decode on 14 of 16 fixtures; it said 129 for the DVB-T clip, which decodes 128 (field-coded: 252 packets, its last picture is an unpaired field that only a decoder drops), and 150 for the raw reset TS (the reset again). The app's count (tasks #24/#26) already handles both.

The byte start decodes from the key before the target instead of a whole GOP earlier, so it saves ~20–40 % of the decoder's CPU on a PAL DVD (12-frame GOPs); in wall time that is 10–40 ms per preview, inside the run-to-run noise on this busy machine. A per-seek window costs more than it saves. No 1080i broadcast TS was available, where #169 measured the preroll's cost at larger GOPs.

7. Recommendation

Criterion (fixed in the plan)Result
1. Cost on TS/PS ≤ 1.2× today's scan, SSD and sharemet (1.00×, 1.10× CPU)
2. Exact wherever today is, no fixture worsemet for M2 on TS/PS and M1 elsewhere
3. Retires C1 and C3/C4, net code removednot met: C1 only, net ≈ 0

Don't build it. The index does what it promises where a whole-file pass already exists, but the corrections it was meant to replace are mostly not about position: C2/C4 are about display order, which only a decoder knows; C6/C7 are about -copyts and resets, which a byte start doesn't touch; C3 is about formatting seconds, which every remaining time seek still does. The one it removes (C1) is exact today on every fixture and the 2 h title.

Where it would be worth reopening: a TS/PS whose GOP later in the file is longer than the first 20 s show (the SeekPlan measures only those). A byte start doesn't care. No such source was found or built here; if one turns up, the cheap form is the merged pass (integer timestamps, key packets only, ~430 kB for 2 h) with M2, for TS/PS alone.

8. Limits

  • One Mac (Apple Silicon), FFmpeg 9.0.2 from the bundle, one share on one network (≈ 60 MB/s, cached reads detected by speed); no Windows client, no wired 1 Gbit or 10 Gbit link. On a faster link the share numbers fall toward the SSD's and the CPU ratio (1.10×) is what remains.
  • The machine ran another task's heavy tests throughout (load average 30–126): wall times on the internal disk vary up to 2×, CPU times are the comparison.
  • One real DVD title (no resets, no PTS-less key packets — the small PS fixture has 3) and one real 1080p MKV per role; the fixtures are synthetic. A 1080i broadcast TS was not available.
  • "Exact" means the luma of 3 (5 on the title) pictures matches; it says nothing about colour.
  • The new-code count is an estimate (2× the Python), the retired count is measured.

9. Not tested, and decisions

  • Not tested: subfile instead of -skip_initial_bytes; a PTS wrap (33-bit) inside a capture — no fixture crosses one, and a byte start decoding with -copyts sees the raw PTS, so a grid origin before the wrap would need the index to unwrap; giving a no-grid PS a grid from the index (it would retire the PS time trim, and would change frame numbering checks #174 made); M2 for audio; a GOP that grows after 20 s.
  • Decision: the real DVD on the share, read-only, instead of the plan's synthetic 2 h title written to the share: a real title answers more, and nothing was written there. The SSD copy was deleted afterwards.
  • Decision: no sudo purge: cold-cache runs on the internal SSD were not measured (nobody to type the password); the share's cold runs carry the IO question and the SSD's CPU times the parse question.
  • Decision: I-key's size is measured, its build is not: ffprobe reads every packet whatever is printed, so I-key costs I-full's read; only the stored size differs (426 kB vs 6.7 MB).
  • Filed separately: today's reset scan blocks analysis for the whole file's read: 77 s for this title on the share (17.3 s/GB), 7 s on the SSD. Not this probe's question, but a measured cost of the current code: task #55.
