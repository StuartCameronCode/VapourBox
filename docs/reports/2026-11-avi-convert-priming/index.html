Convert into AVI: a null frame at the head and the sound off by the encoder's priming

2026-11-15 · macOS arm64, deps 1.16.0 (FFmpeg 9.0.2) · task #38, found during the AVI late video fix (task #30)

Outcome: fixed. Every lossy audio encoder starts with a few samples of start-up, its priming (encoder delay): AAC 1024 samples, libmp3lame 1105, AC-3 256. Its first packet has a negative timestamp, and MP4, MOV and MKV mark it to be skipped (an edit list, skip samples). AVI cannot. ffmpeg's default -avoid_negative_ts auto then shifted every stream later by the priming. At 25 fps that rounds to a whole frame for AAC and MP3, so avienc wrote a null frame at the head (101 stored frames for 100 pictures; small_clip.mp4 0..49 stored 51). The player then played the priming as sound, so the sound was off by the priming minus the video's shift: AAC −18.6 ms, MP3 −16.9 ms, AC-3 +5.4 ms, on every source, gap or not.

For Convert into AVI the worker now measures the priming from the job's own encoder, reads every encoded sound input that much later, and writes -avoid_negative_ts disabled. AAC, MP3 and AC-3 then store 100 frames and sit 0.08–0.10 ms from the beep, the same as the PCM control. The cost is the first 21–23 ms of sound, which is replaced by the encoder's near-silent start-up. MKV, MP4, MOV and Passthrough are unchanged, byte for byte.

Fixtures and method

The late-video fixtures of Tests/TestResources/generate_video_start_offset.sh: 25 fps, 100 pictures, the video starting 1 s after the sound, white frames at pictures 25 and 75 with a 1 kHz beep at each, all mono 48 kHz. late_video.mp4 holds AAC, late_video_intra.ts MP2 and late_video.mkv FLAC. The fault does not depend on the gap: small_clip.mp4 (29.97 fps, stereo AAC, no gap) shows it too. Every job runs through the worker with FFV1 into AVI and Convert at 192 kbps, the app's default quality.

Lag is the beep's onset minus the white frame's presentation time, both decoded from the output. The beep's time is counted in decoded samples from the stream's start (scripts/measure.py, as in the #30 report). Stored frames is the AVI header's avih.dwTotalFrames, read directly by scripts/riff.py, which agrees with the video strh.dwLength and ffprobe's nb_frames in every row. A null frame counts there but is not decoded, so the decoded count stayed 100 throughout and only the stored count shows the fault.

The priming, per encoder

scripts/priming.py encodes 0.2 s of silence and reads the first packet's timestamp from -f framecrc. This is the same probe the worker runs (audio_restoration::priming_probe_args).

The count in samples is constant, but the encoder picks its own rate: MP3 and AC-3 resample a 96 kHz input to 48 kHz. That is why the worker measures the priming in seconds from the encoder's time base and does not use a table indexed by the source rate.

The MKV rows are off by the fixture's own seek, not by this change. The PCM control reads −2.58 ms before and after. An input -ss on this MKV (FLAC in 21 ms packets with millisecond timestamps) is not sample-exact: through plain ffmpeg, PCM seeked 0, 5, 10 and 21.33 ms past the first picture lands at −2.58, −4.58, −9.58 and −20.58 ms, where the MP4 tracks the seek to within 0.1 ms. The +0.75 ms after the fix is therefore within that seek error, and closer to 0 than the control.

Cells are stored frames and lag (ms). Stopping the shift removes the null frame but leaves the sound late by the priming, which the player still plays. Seeking the encoder's input P later cancels it, so decoded time t plays source time t. -itsoffset has no effect beside an input -ss.

Decisions

  • disabled over setts. Both measured the same. -avoid_negative_ts disabled is one output option with no per-stream numbers, and avienc ignores where an audio stream starts, so a seek one sample short cannot bring a shift back. With setts, a first packet at −1 would trigger the shift again. In AVI the video starts at 0 (task #30), no subtitles are mapped, and under Convert every audio stream is encoded and seeked alike. On an IVTC/decimating job the option replaces make_zero.
  • The priming is measured, not tabled. The encoder picks its own rate (see the priming table above). FLAC and PCM measure none and change nothing. A failed probe is a warning, never a refusal.
  • Only the encoder's inputs move (audio_restoration::encode_sound_start): the source, the NUT sound copy, and the restored WAV (-ss P). source_audio_start is unchanged, so the render, transcription and notes keep the true start. An untrimmed delayed AVI's sound seek moves off 0 and therefore takes the NUT copy (rule 9). integration_avi_audio_seek_test.dart checks this on the chirp fixture: before, 63 stored frames for 62 and 1024 samples off; after, 62 and within 2 samples.
  • Not PCM in place of the user's codec. Swapping the codec overrides an explicit choice and makes the file about 8× larger, and MP3, the obvious "compatible" substitute, has the same fault.
  • Not an -af. The encode never filters audio (the input seek does the same job).

Found on the way: AAC packets over avienc's block size (task #41, not fixed)

The first run of the heavy test failed on late_video.mkv and a trimmed late_video_gop.ts, ~21 ms late at one flash. That test reads beep times from each decoded frame's timestamp, whereas measure.py counts samples. The cause is a separate fault. avienc writes AAC's nBlockAlign as 768 bytes per channel, and a reader of VBR AVI audio counts a chunk as ceil(size / nBlockAlign) packets. ffmpeg's AAC encoder at 192 kbps mono occasionally writes 769–777 bytes, and from that chunk on ffmpeg's reader times the sound one packet (21.33 ms) late. The file holds every chunk (checked chunk by chunk). In 20 mono encodes per bitrate, 96, 128 and 160 kbps never exceeded 768 bytes and 192 kbps did once. The last column of worker_convert_avi.py's output counts such packets: one, on the MKV AAC row (its sample-counted lag above is unaffected). The heavy tests run Convert at Medium (128 kbps) so that they measure this fault and not that one. Filed as task #41.

Limits

  • macOS arm64 only. avienc and the encoders are the same FFmpeg 9.0 series on every platform, and the nightly runs cover them.
  • Sync is measured with ffmpeg's decoders. Other players are assumed to play the priming too, since nothing in an AVI says to skip it.
  • The first P of sound (21–23 ms at 44.1/48 kHz) is the encoder's start-up, not the source's.
  • All encoded streams share one P, measured at the rate of the first sound encoded (the restored track's, else the source's first stream's). A second stream at another rate is off by the difference (< 3 ms between 44.1 and 48 kHz AAC).
  • A source with millisecond audio timestamps (the MKV rows) seeks only to within a few ms, before and after.
  • Opus cannot be written into AVI at all (task #39). The AAC block-size fault is task #41.
